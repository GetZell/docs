## Overview

Call Analysis is the detail view for a single call. It combines recording playback, transcript context, evaluation results, feedback comments, inline coaching, and navigation from snippets or history.

## Real-Call Header Metadata

For real calls, the header displays recipient identity, company, date, duration, language, call type, outcome, and owner assignment when those values exist.

Role behavior:

- `USER`: sees language, call type, and outcome as read-only metadata.
- `TEAM_LEAD`: can update language, call type, and outcome from inline header controls.
- `ADMIN` and `INTERNAL_TOOL_ADMIN`: can update language, call type, outcome, title, and owner assignment.

Language, call type, outcome, and owner changes are optimistic in the frontend and only show toast notifications on errors. Successful saves are silent. Title changes still show success and error toasts.

## Navigation Behavior

Call Analysis preserves breadcrumb context when opened from call collections or hiring flows. If a call cannot be loaded after deletion or missing data, the route redirects admins to team reports and regular users to home.

## Recording Playback

When a call includes video, Call Analysis displays the recording in a floating player alongside the shared waveform controls. Users can drag the player by its top grip and resize it from the bottom-left corner grip. The player starts at 320 pixels wide, with a usual minimum of 240 pixels. Its maximum size follows both the available viewport width and height instead of a fixed 640-pixel cap. It can shrink below the minimum in a smaller viewport. With the resize handle focused, the left and right arrow keys change the width in 20-pixel steps.

Moving the pointer over the video or focusing its controls reveals buttons to rewind 10 seconds, play or pause, and move forward 10 seconds. Controls hide after three seconds without activity, even while paused or with the pointer resting over the video. They stay visible during pointer interaction or while a control has keyboard focus. Clicking the video also toggles playback. Rewind and forward actions stop at the beginning or end of the recording and preserve the current playing or paused state.

The video timeline supports seeking without toggling playback and immediately updates the synchronized transcript. Feedback markers use the same blue dots as the audio waveform. Hovering over or focusing a marker shows its label and timestamp; selecting it seeks to that moment. The central playback buttons hide while the marker tooltip is open so they do not overlap it.

The bottom-right fullscreen button expands the player immediately and requests browser fullscreen. If browser fullscreen is unsupported, denied, or slow to activate, the player remains expanded within the page with its custom timeline and controls. Supported WebKit browsers can use native video fullscreen instead. The same playback element stays mounted. The exit button or Escape returns from the expanded view, and browser fullscreen exit events also restore the floating player.

Before playback, a separate video decoder attempts to show a still from the recording. The preview may come from a later frame, but the playback position remains at 0:00. Playing or seeking dismisses the preview and cancels any pending preview work.

The waveform controls remain available for video and audio recordings. They show the current time and duration and provide playback speed, volume, waveform seeking, and play or pause controls.

### Black Preview Troubleshooting

- The preview decoder checks several later frames and skips frames whose sampled pixels are almost entirely black. It stops after finding a visible frame, exhausting its candidates, or reaching a 15-second deadline. Recordings with long black sections or slow media loading may still have no preview.
- If the initial media request fails, the decoder retries without CORS and seeks to 30 seconds, or halfway through a shorter recording. It cannot inspect pixels in this fallback, so the selected frame can still be black. A redirected response can also prevent pixel inspection.
- Preview decoding is separate from the playback element. A preview error or timeout does not block normal playback. When investigating a black preview, first check whether pressing Play displays video; distinguish a preview-only problem from a recording that also stays black during playback.
